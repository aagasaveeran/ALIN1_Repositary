[Integrity]
[Topic: Defining Morality, Ethics, and Integrity in RTL]
MORALITY: Defined in RTL as the principles that differentiate right/wrong and good/bad behavior by individuals[cite: 892].
ETHICAL: Defined as the principles and standards that differentiate right/wrong and good/bad conduct by an organization or system[cite: 893].
INTEGRITY (RTL DEFINITION): Being "whole" and undiminished, which allows for the manifestation of one's full potential[cite: 895, 901]. Integrity is not a judgment of "good" or "bad" people; instead, actions are simply viewed as being "in" or "out" of integrity[cite: 903].

[Topic: The Practice of Integrity]
Integrity involves a three-step internal process to bridge gaps in performance and values:
* Noticing gaps without judging oneself[cite: 905].
* Stating the fact of the gap without guilt or praise[cite: 906].
* Taking action to narrow that gap in everyday life at work, home, and in society[cite: 907, 908].
TRUE INTEGRITY ALSO MEANS:
* Creating a new future through one's speaking and actions rather than suppressing life[cite: 913].
* Creating space for people to source their deepest selves instead of shrinking that space[cite: 914].

[Topic: The Three Levels of Integrity and the CFSR Model]
Integrity is practiced across the three layers of the Conscious Full-Spectrum Response (CFSR) Model[cite: 927]:
1. TECHNICAL LEVEL (Doing):
   a. I do what I say[cite: 916, 931].
   b. I deliver on what I plan to accomplish in my action plan[cite: 917, 932].
2. SYSTEMS & CULTURAL LEVEL (Inquiring):
   a. I inquire into the congruence between my actions/strategies and universal values/principles[cite: 918, 933].
   b. I speak up and speak out responsibly based on what I stand for, distinguished from emotional reactions[cite: 919, 934, 972].
3. TRANSFORMATIONAL LEVEL (Being):
   a. I am the change and the shift I wish to see[cite: 920, 937].
   b. I have the courage to create[cite: 921, 938].


[Topic: Integrity Exercise 1 - Results vs. Reasons]
EXAMINATION OF COMMITMENT: Think of a recent situation where you were expected to act (e.g., participating in a call or contacting a family member)[cite: 949, 953].
* Reflection: State to a partner, "I was [in or out of] Integrity," without providing reasons or justifications[cite: 945, 946, 951, 954].
* Awareness: In life, we either have reasons or results[cite: 947].
* Future Action: Reflect on how you felt and what you will do differently in the future to stay in integrity[cite: 952, 955, 961].

[Topic: Integrity Exercise 2 - Congruence with Values]
EXAMINATION OF ALIGNMENT: Review your current project's actions and strategies using the CFSR worksheet[cite: 969, 974].
* Inquiry: Are these actions truly congruent with the universal values and principles you stand for[cite: 975]?
* Modification: Identify what you need to modify, add, or delete to bring your project back into integrity with those values[cite: 976].
