[Background Conversations and Listening]
[Topic: Background Conversations - Definition and Awareness]
BACKGROUND CONVERSATIONS: The constant internal dialogue that human beings have, whether they are consciously aware of it or not[cite: 162]. Being aware of these internal conversations provides an individual with greater freedom of thought and action[cite: 163].

[Topic: The Choice in Leadership]
LEADERSHIP CHOICE: A fundamental decision in the RTL framework regarding how one manages internal dialogue. An effective leader chooses to notice their background conversations and listen deeply to others rather than allowing those internal conversations to drive their behavior[cite: 167, 221].

[Topic: Deep Listening vs. Obedience]
DEEP LISTENING: In the RTL context, listening is not defined as "obedience," as it is in many cultures or school systems[cite: 172]. Instead, it is the ability to listen deeply to what someone is saying without judgment[cite: 173].

[Topic: Identifying Common Background Conversations]
TYPES OF INTERNAL DIALOGUE: When listening to others, common background conversations that can interfere include:
* Right/Wrong or Agree/Disagree assessments[cite: 187, 188].
* "Find the Flaw" thinking[cite: 189].
* Feelings of being "Not Responsible"[cite: 190].
* Us/Them or Either/Or categorizations[cite: 191, 192].
* Thoughts of "Not Enough"[cite: 193].
Individuals are encouraged to notice and write down these thoughts to increase self-awareness[cite: 194].

[Topic: The Emotional Impact of Distracted Listening]
DISTRACTED LISTENING: Occurs when a listener is preoccupied, uninterested, or multi-tasking. This style leads to a breakdown in trust and personal value.
Associated Negative Feelings:
* Extreme Feelings: Feeling like a waste of time, irritated, or ignored.
* Social/Relational: Disconnected, disrespected, disgusted, judged, or neglected.
* Self-Worth: Feeling worthless, small, less valued, humiliated, or embarrassed.
* Physical/Mental State: Angry, annoyed, avoided, disappointed, disturbed, or frustrated.

[Topic: The Emotional Impact of Deep Listening]
DEEP LISTENING (IMPACT): Characterized by present, empathetic, and active engagement with the speaker. This fosters psychological safety, bonding, and mutual respect.
Associated Positive Feelings:
* Extreme Feelings: Happy, totally valued, and totally connected.
* Relational: Bonding, totally supported, trusted, loved, and totally respected.
* Empowerment: Feeling powerful, having a strong bond, feeling encouraged, and experiencing freedom.
* Mental State: Relaxed, relieved, grounded, grateful, fulfilled, and interested.

[Topic: Summary of Listening as a Leadership Tool]
SUMMARY OF IMPACT: Deep listening is a transformative leadership tool that shifts a person’s state from feeling "Worthless" or like a "Waste of time" (due to distracted listening) to feeling "Valued," "Supported," and "Grounded."

[Topic: The Art of Listening (William Stringfellow)]
LISTENING AS AN ART: As described by William Stringfellow, listening is a "primitive art of love" where a person makes themselves accessible and vulnerable to another's word[cite: 213]. True listening cannot occur if a person is preoccupied with their own appearance, trying to impress the other, or deciding what to say next[cite: 206, 208, 209].

[Topic: Inspiration for Speaking Up]
THINGS THAT MATTER: As stated by Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., "Our lives begin to end on the day we become silent about the things that matter"[cite: 224].
