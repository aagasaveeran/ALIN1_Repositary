[Topic: Courage vs. Bravery - Definitions]
COURAGE (RTL DEFINITION): A strong inner capacity where strategic action is sourced from the "heart" (from the Latin 'cor')—specifically one's universal values and what they stand for[cite: 773, 775]. Originally, courage meant "To speak one's mind by telling all one's heart"[cite: 774].
BRAVERY (RTL DEFINITION): Defined as being "daring"[cite: 777]. Historically, it stems from "braverie," meaning daring, defiance, or boasting[cite: 776]. In modern society, bravery is often defined by cultural norms or formal systems and is not necessarily based on equity and dignity for all[cite: 777].
DISTINCTION: While often used interchangeably today, RTL distinguishes courage as value-based action, whereas bravery can be based on social pressure or defiance of others[cite: 777, 778].

[Topic: Transcending Fear for Courageous Action]
ACTING IN SPITE OF FEAR: Courage is defined not as the absence of fear, but as the ability to act in spite of it[cite: 789].
THE FOUR-STEP PROCESS:
* Notice the fear[cite: 790].
* Name the fear[cite: 791].
* Let go/Release the fear[cite: 792].
* Be in action sourcing universal values[cite: 793].
