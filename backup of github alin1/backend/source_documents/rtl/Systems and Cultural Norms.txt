[Systems and Cultural Norms]
[Topic: Defining Systems for Meaningful Participation]
SYSTEM (DEFINITION): A set of connected, interacting, interrelated, or interdependent elements forming a complex whole[cite: 532]. It can also be defined as a set of principles or processes according to which a function is performed or something is accomplished[cite: 533].
DEMYSTIFYING THEORY: In RTL, the goal is to define "system" so everyone can relate to it without being an expert systems analyst[cite: 530, 531]. 
EXAMPLE: The human circulatory system consists of connected parts—heart, arteries, capillaries, and veins—all moving blood throughout the body[cite: 534].

[Topic: Cultural Norms - Categories and Examples]
CULTURAL NORMS: Rules and expectations of behavior and thought based on shared beliefs within a specific social or cultural group[cite: 538].
RTL categorizes norms into three types:
1. LIFE-ENHANCING: Norms that promote well-being, such as nutritious food, sleep, exercise, meditation, and being with nature[cite: 540, 546].
2. NEUTRAL: Contextual traditions, such as wearing specific dress for formal functions or putting a coin in an empty box before returning it[cite: 541, 547, 548].
3. DIMINISHING OR EXPLOITATIVE: Norms that sustain inequity, such as expecting mothers to be selfless at the cost of their health, caste-based restrictions on cleaning, or bullying disabled children[cite: 542, 549].

[Topic: Radical Systems and Cultural Transformation]
RADICAL TRANSFORMATION vs. INCREMENTAL CHANGE: The Conscious Full-Spectrum Response (CFSR) model is used to generate radical transformation rather than minor changes[cite: 558]. 
KEY TRANSFORMATIONAL ACTIONS:
* Activation of the "pattern mind" and critical thinking to see visible and invisible working elements[cite: 560].
* Seeing the "invisible rules of the game" and connecting dots while grounded in dignity, equity, and compassion[cite: 561].
* Creating new formations and patterns grounded in universal values[cite: 569].
* Recognizing interdependence as a core value for sustainable change[cite: 574].
