[Discernment vs Judgement]
[Topic: Discernment and Judgement - Definitions]
DISCERNMENT (DEFINITION): The ability to think critically while being informed by universal values such as compassion, dignity, equity, and fairness[cite: 852, 853]. Cultivating discernment is essential for making universal values the basis of strategic action[cite: 853].
JUDGEMENT (DEFINITION): In the RTL context (non-legal sense), judgement refers to looking at situations through the lens of right/wrong, us/them, or blame/shame[cite: 854, 856]. It often leads to polarized opinions, self-righteous behavior, and continuous internal or external badgering[cite: 855].

[Topic: The Practical Impact of Discernment vs. Judgement]
OPENING vs. CLOSING POSSIBILITIES: Distinguishing between these two states is considered both freeing and practical for practitioners[cite: 856].
* Impact of Discernment: Noticing when one is discerning opens up new possibilities and leads to considered decisions[cite: 857]. It is described as being aligned with values, clear, empathic, authentic, and grounded in love and courage[cite: 859].
* Impact of Judgement: Judging oneself or others closes down options and spaces for new futures[cite: 858, 862]. It leaves individuals feeling self-righteous, guilty, shamed, justified, frustrated, anxious, boxed-in, resentful, and unproductive[cite: 861].

[Topic: Exercise 1 - The Feeling of Being Judged]
EXAMINATION OF EXTERNAL JUDGEMENT: Reflect on an incident at home or work where you said something you disagreed with, and someone reacted by judging you[cite: 864, 865].
* How did you feel in that moment? [cite: 866]
* Has that feeling stayed with you over time? [cite: 866]

[Topic: Exercise 2 - The Feeling of Being Discerned]
EXAMINATION OF VALUE-BASED CONNECTION: Reflect on an incident where you disagreed with someone, but they were able to still themselves and connect with you based on universal values[cite: 869, 870].
* How did you feel when the person discerned rather than judged? [cite: 871]
* How has that feeling impacted your ongoing relationship or state of mind? [cite: 871]

[Topic: Exercise 3 - Internal Practice of Discernment]
SHIFTING FROM JUDGEMENT TO VALUES:
* Part A (Judgement): Think of an incident where you judged another person. What could you do differently next time to shift that internal conversation? [cite: 873, 874]
* Part B (Discernment): Think of an incident where you stilled yourself and connected with someone based on universal values. What will you do to continue this practice? [cite: 875, 876, 877]
